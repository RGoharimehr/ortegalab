    American Refining and Biochemical Geowell Simulator Research Project
    Project Sponsor:

    ARB Geowell, Albert Koenig

    Project Team:

    Alfonso Ortega, Kevin Woods, Thomas Baldassare

    Background:

    HVAC systems are large consumers of electrical energy especially during the summer months during heat waves which strain the electrical grid and cause brown-outs. The research project focuses on using the earth as a heat sink during the summer and a heat source during the winter by pumping ground water out for HVAC purposes and returning it back into the ground. This system uses a small pump running on electrical power to run groundwater through a heat exchanger to heat and cool a house. Since ground water stays at a relatively constant temperature throughout the year due to the large heat capacity of the earth and large mass of water underground, a building can be kept at a constant temperature throughout the year alleviating the need for large boilers and air conditioners.


    Project Objectives:

    There were three main objectives of this research project. The first was to build a test simulator for a scaled-down version of a geothermal water well using components used in the field. The second was to manufacture a new type of inlet apparatus which would be situated at the bottom of a typical well and the third objective was to capture the flow of the water through the inlet apparatus to understand the fluid dynamics of the system. The components of the system are the column (above) with the submerged pump, float and weight inserted and the control panel (right) with a variable frequency drive to control the pump, a solenoid gate valve to open the system when running and a rotameter for the flow rate.
