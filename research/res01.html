Heat Transfer and Fluid Dynamics in a Liquid Droplet Impinging on a Surface

Project Team


Alfonso Ortega, Mahsa Ebrahim and Andres Diaz

Project Sponsor


The James R. Birle Endowment

Motivation

 

With the exponential increase in chip power-densities, new innovative techniques must be found in which the thermal loads are effectively dissipated. Traditional air cooling mechanisms in many applications are not an efficient solution to the problem and numerous liquid based cooling mechanisms have been developed including mini and micro-channel closed loop systems, sweat cooling of porous media, and spray cooling. Spray cooling, while investigated extensively, is still not fully understood; this being from the ad hoc approach from the industrial sector.

Project Description


This work seeks to investigate the physics of droplet impingement and thin film evaporation to fully understand the thermal and fluid phenomena involved in this widely growing field. A carrier gas is seeded with a droplet which impinges on a test surface and high speed photography captures the spreading and receding of the thin film formed by the droplet. The effect of the surface temperature and Reynolds number are investigated in order to fully understand all of the scaling aspects of this unique problem.

Experimental results are compared with computational results found with the VOF model showing good agreement between the two. Simple scale analysis has shown a very promising increase in the cooling efficiency of thin film evaporation and thus leads the research team to believe that this synergistic method of combining the liquid and gas phase cooling mechanisms has great potential as a cooling system.
