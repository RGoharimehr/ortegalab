Simulation of Two-Phase Flow and Heat Transfer in Mini/Microchannel Heat Exchangers

Project Team


Alfonso Ortega and Devin Pellicone

Project Sponsor


Quanta Cool Inc.

Motivation

 

The ever growing need for mini and microscale electronic devices drives the need for smaller and more effective means of dissipating their heat. Microchannels, on their own, provide the means for large heat transfer coefficients due to the inverse proportionality of the Nusselt number with respect to the hydraulic diameter. For high heat flux situations, single phase forced convection in microchannels may not provide the necessary heat dissipation. Flow boiling in microchannels presents a viable solution by exploiting the energy dissipation potential in the latent heat of vaporization of a fluid. In the single phase regime, commercial CFD approaches allow the use of conjugate approaches, in which the fluid side momentum equation and the fluid and solid side energy equations are solved simultaneously. By now, such approaches are routine and their practical utilization for engineering design is limited only by the length of time required for execution of large problems. On the other hand, heat exchangers that operate in the multi-phase regime cannot be solved using a conjugate approach, because it is impractical to solve the full flow and energy equations using a computational approach. As such, many simplifications have been proposed for performing approximate, semi-empirical computations for multi-phase flow in channels. There does not appear to be a "best practice" for practical modeling of two-phase flow and heat transfer in micro-channel heat exchangers and this is the principal motivation for the present work.
